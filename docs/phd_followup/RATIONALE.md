# Why keep a calculation-mistake comparison?

Draft revision: 16 November 2026. Wording and launch approval are still pending.
No API run or human recruitment was performed for this revision.

## Recommendation

Keep unauthorized AI use and deliberately made-up results as the integrity cases.
Keep a calculation mistake as a proposed competence comparison, subject to a
short human wording review. It asks whether a disproved concern about the quality
of someone's work carries a different penalty from a disproved dishonesty claim.
It is not another example of academic misconduct.

The prior “program-test” wording was unnecessarily specialized. The new cases
describe a course report using supplied data. One alleges invented results;
the other alleges using the wrong method to calculate an average. A single mistake could
signal carelessness, unfamiliarity, or ordinary human error rather than low
ability. The label alone cannot establish what readers infer.

## What the literature supports

Haesevoets, Reinders Folmer, and Brebels [1] reported four hiring experiments.
Their integrity accusations left a disadvantage after refutation, whereas their
competence accusations did not show the same residual pattern. In Study 3,
competence involved repeatedly using incorrect product codes; integrity involved using work
time for personal purposes. They also measured perceived competence and integrity.
The accepted manuscript was checked, including methods and manipulation checks.
This supports testing the distinction, not treating our coursework example as
already validated. Their staged trust judgments also differ from our single
application containing the complete history.
The coursework draft specifies a wrong calculation method rather than a typo;
nevertheless, one coursework error is a weaker ability cue than repeated job errors.

Kim et al. [2] found that the success of apology versus denial depended on whether
the allegation concerned competence or integrity, and on later evidence of guilt
or innocence. The published abstract was checked. This motivates attention to
the content of the allegation but does not establish an effect of institutional
acknowledgment: the applicant's response and the institution's response differ.

Greitemeyer and Sagioglou [3] found that exonerated researchers were evaluated
similarly to controls in their experiments. Their second study also showed why
controls must carry comparable positive information: merely learning that someone
had authored a paper improved evaluations. The full article was checked. Its
nonsignificant exoneration-control contrast should not be treated as proof of
equivalence. Human findings do not justify predicting a universal residual penalty.

## Revised wording choices

| Earlier draft | Revised draft | Reason and tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| “Program-test results” and “original program-test logs” | Results, calculations, and data supplied for a course report | Removes unexplained programming terminology and identifies the evidence being checked. |
| Special routine review by an integrity office or university panel | Instructor checks work during normal grading for the class | More plausible course process; evidence can still add positive information. AI verification and clearing retain different authorities. |
| Zero for an honest mistake after a hearing | 10 marks deducted from a 100-mark report under normal grading rules | A plausible draft grading consequence, not a penalty selected or validated by the literature. It differs from fabrication's zero. |
| Misconduct-style appeal for an honest mistake | Departmental grade review of the original calculation; marks restored | Separates correction of a false concern from improvement of genuinely incorrect work. |
| Institutional acknowledgment in formal language | New cases use “confirmed in writing that its original ... decision was wrong” | Uses the actual decision maker. It remains exactly one extra sentence beyond the appeal/grade-review version. |

All seven repeated conditions retain their original text. The other 13 conditions
(26 wordings) are revised. Stable internal IDs such as `technical_error` remain
for analysis compatibility; participants and models do not see these IDs.

## What this design can establish

Within each case, compare clearing with a no-prior-concern condition that supplies
favorable evidence about the same work. Also compare the otherwise-identical
appeal with and without a written acknowledgment. These are more informative than
treating any difference from the short clean control as a residual accusation cost.
Authority, length, and wording still vary in the clearing-control comparison.

Keep cross-case differences as a qualified comparison of these particular
stories. They do not isolate a causal effect of integrity versus competence:
consequences, institutions, reassurance, and perceived seriousness also differ.
The 36-comparison advancement family and the secondary cross-case comparisons
remain unchanged in the draft analysis; this wording revision does not promote
any mechanism claim or change statistical families after seeing results.

One case per topic and two paraphrases do not provide independent samples of
allegation types. The same 100 applicants improve precision across applicants,
but cannot establish generality across scenarios. Direct claims that models
differ from humans require humans to judge the same materials under a comparable
design. This follow-up cannot measure sequential belief updating.

## Before freezing the materials

Use a small comprehension review, for example 5–10 readers as a practical starting
point, rather than treating that number as sufficient statistical validation.
Do not show the same reader every condition side by side when assessing how it
is interpreted in isolation. Ask readers to identify:

- What allegedly happened, who checked it, and whether it actually occurred.
- Whether the concern primarily suggests dishonesty, weak ability, or carelessness
  (rate these separately; they can overlap).
- How serious the concern seems and whether the grading/review process is plausible.
- Whether clearing establishes that the original work was correct, rather than
  that the applicant fixed a real mistake.
- Whether the control sounds like ordinary grading, without an unstated accusation.

Resolve recurring confusion before collection. If the mistake is seen as too
trivial or as a hidden dishonesty allegation, revise it or omit that comparison;
do not select wording because it produces a desired model penalty. If a broad
integrity-versus-competence claim becomes the goal, plan multiple independently
developed cases and a larger validation study. That would be a separate scope
and budget decision.
