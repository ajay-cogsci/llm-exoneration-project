Topic: forecasting from public environmental time series
Question: How does variation in missing-observation frequency affect imputation and short-horizon forecasting on public rainfall measurement series?
Rationale: A low average forecast error may hide failure during extended outages; comparing missingness patterns can clarify when imputation ceases to be useful.
Contribution: Characterize conditions where simple imputation preserves forecast usefulness and where uncertainty or long outages undermine that conclusion.
Method: Compare several imputation methods across missing-observation frequency, retain chronological splits, and evaluate separately by sensor and missingness pattern. Comparison: Compare the resulting ridge forecasts with seasonal-naive forecasts on the same test dates. Also apply the same ridge model to unaltered historical inputs as a reference.
Evaluation: Report forecast errors by sensor and outage pattern over several windows, including the error of the seasonal-naive reference.
Feasibility: Use small public inputs and university computing available through the department; schedule a pilot, comparisons and a written report within six months.
Limitations: Chronological evaluation avoids future-data leakage, while simulated missingness may differ from the process causing actual sensor failures.
