PROPOSED RESEARCH
Area: Applied machine learning
Topic: forecasting from public environmental time series
Question: How does variation in outage duration affect imputation and short-horizon forecasting on public rainfall measurement series?
Rationale: A low average forecast error may hide failure during extended outages; comparing missingness patterns can clarify when imputation ceases to be useful.
Contribution: Characterize conditions where simple imputation preserves forecast usefulness and where uncertainty or long outages undermine that conclusion.
Method: Vary outage duration across chronological evaluation windows, preserve a final holdout period, and compare imputation with seasonal-naive and unaltered-input reference forecasts. Fill missing historical inputs using past observations only, then forecast one step ahead with lagged-input ridge regression fitted only on earlier observations.
Comparison: Compare the resulting ridge forecasts with seasonal-naive forecasts on the same test dates. Also apply the same ridge model to unaltered historical inputs as a reference.
Evaluation: Report sensor-level error distributions and uncertainty across windows; test whether conclusions hold for long contiguous outages in the final holdout period.
Feasibility: Use small public inputs and university computing available through the department; schedule a pilot, comparisons and a written report within six months.
Limitations: Observed sensor failures can have different causes from injected missingness; compare several patterns and avoid interpreting associations as causal outage effects.
